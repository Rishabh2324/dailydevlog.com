## What this course is for

This course helps software engineers understand the ideas and words of the AI field. After it, you can follow AI
discussions at work, read model docs and product announcements, and make sensible choices when you build with AI.

It does not try to turn you into an AI researcher. There is no math to derive and no code to run.

## How lessons work

1. **One idea per lesson.** Each lesson explains one term or concept, and takes about 10 minutes.
2. **Plain English first.** Every idea gets a simple definition and an everyday example before any detail.
3. **Real-life examples.** You'll see how the idea shows up in products and tools you already use.
4. **Depth is optional.** Each lesson ends with sources. Read them only when you want to go deeper.
5. **Key terms.** Every lesson ends with a short table of the words it introduced, so you can review quickly.

## The path

```text
Season 1  Foundations    how models learn, tokens, transformers, LLMs
   ↓
Season 2  LLM apps       prompts, APIs, structured output, chat, evals
   ↓
Season 3  Retrieval      embeddings, search, vector databases
   ↓
Season 4  RAG            answering from your own documents
   ↓
Season 5  Agents         tools, agents, MCP, memory
   ↓
Season 6  Quality        evals, security, observability
   ↓
Season 7  Production     architecture, cost, shipping
   ↓
Season 8  Under the hood open models, fine-tuning, multimodal
   ↓
Season 9  Frontier       coding agents and new ideas
```

Seasons 1 to 4 are the core. If you only have time for part of the course, read those.

<Callout type="opinion" title="What you can skip">
  You don't need calculus, linear algebra, PyTorch or GPU programming to work well with AI as a software engineer.
  Learn those only if you move into training models yourself.
</Callout>
