## Why this matters

"Generative AI" is everywhere, but the word "generative" has a specific meaning. Most AI before ChatGPT was the
other kind: models that sort things into categories. Knowing both kinds helps you pick the right tool for a task.

By the end of this lesson, you'll be able to tell whether a task needs a model that picks or a model that creates.

## The idea

A **discriminative model** answers "which one is this?" It looks at an input and picks a label. Spam or not spam.
Cat or dog. Fraud or safe.

A **generative model** learns what the data itself looks like. Because it knows that, it can create new examples
that look real: a new sentence, a new image, a new song.

```text
Discriminative:  email  →  "spam"        (picks a label)
Generative:      "Write a thank-you email" → a new email
```

## A real-life example

Think of two people at an art museum.

The first is a **security guard trained to spot fakes**. Show them a painting and they say "real" or "fake". They
don't need to know how to paint. They only need to notice the differences. That's a discriminative model.

The second is an **art student who has studied thousands of paintings** in a style. They can paint a brand-new
picture in that style. To do that, they had to learn what the style really looks like. That's a generative model.

In real products: your **bank's fraud check** is discriminative. It decides "risky" or "normal" for each payment.
**Image generators like Midjourney** and **chatbots like Claude** are generative. They create new images and text.

## How it works

1. A discriminative model learns the **boundary** between categories. It only cares about what separates spam from
   normal email.
2. A generative model learns the **patterns of the data** itself: which words tend to follow which, what faces
   usually look like.
3. Once a generative model knows those patterns, it can sample new data, one piece at a time. An LLM picks one word
   after another (lesson 1.4.2).
4. Generative models can also classify. You can ask an LLM "Is this email spam?" and it will answer. That's often
   easier to set up, but slower and more expensive than a small discriminative model.

## Where you'll see it

- **Classification tasks** (tagging tickets, detecting fraud, filtering content) often use small discriminative
  models, because they are cheap and fast.
- **Creation tasks** (writing, summarizing, drawing, coding) need generative models.
- **Teams now often prototype with an LLM** for classification, then switch to a smaller model if the volume grows.
- **"GenAI" in product names** means the generative kind.

## Common confusions

- **Generative doesn't mean smarter.** For a narrow yes/no task, a small discriminative model can be more accurate
  and much cheaper.
- **An LLM answering "spam" is still a generative model.** It generates the word "spam". The task is
  classification, but the model type is generative.
- **Generative models don't copy their training data.** They learn patterns and produce new combinations, though
  they can sometimes repeat things they saw often.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Discriminative model | A model that picks a label for an input |
| Generative model | A model that learns the data well enough to create new examples |
| Classification | The task of sorting inputs into categories |
| Sampling | Producing new output from what a generative model learned |
