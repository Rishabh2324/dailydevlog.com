## Why this matters

The last lesson said training "lowers the loss". Gradient descent is how. It's the engine behind almost every
neural network, including every LLM. Words like "learning rate", "epoch" and "optimizer" all come from it.

By the end of this lesson, you'll be able to explain how a model improves itself, step by step.

## The idea

**Gradient descent** repeats one simple loop:

1. Measure the loss.
2. Work out the **gradient**: for each parameter, which way makes the loss go up, and how steeply.
3. Take a **small step** the opposite way, so the loss goes down.
4. Repeat, many thousands or millions of times.

The size of each step is the **learning rate**.

## A real-life example

A common way to picture it: you're **walking down a hill in thick fog**. You can't see the valley. But you can feel
the ground under your feet. So you feel which way slopes down, take a step that way, and feel again. Step by step,
you reach the bottom.

- The height of the hill is the loss.
- Feeling the slope is computing the gradient.
- The length of your step is the learning rate. Tiny steps take forever. Giant leaps can jump right over the
  valley.

Another everyday version: **adjusting a shower's temperature**. Too cold, turn it a bit warmer. Too hot, turn it
back a little. If you turn the knob too far each time, you keep swinging between freezing and burning. That's a
learning rate that's too high.

## How it works

1. **Start with random parameters.** The model's answers are bad and the loss is high.
2. **Look at a batch of examples.** Using all the data at once is too slow, so training uses small groups called
   **batches**. This version is called **stochastic gradient descent (SGD)**.
3. **Compute the gradient** for every parameter: should it go up or down, and how much does it matter? For neural
   networks, backpropagation does this (lesson 1.1.8).
4. **Update every parameter** a little, in the direction that lowers the loss.
5. **Repeat.** One full pass through the training data is an **epoch**. Big models see their data about once;
   small models may go through it many times.

Modern training uses improved versions of this loop called **optimizers**. The most common one is **Adam**
(Kingma & Ba, 2014), which adjusts the step size for each parameter automatically.

## Where you'll see it

- **Fine-tuning settings:** learning rate, batch size and number of epochs are the first options you'll set.
- **Training logs:** a loss that goes down smoothly is good; a loss that jumps around or explodes usually means the
  learning rate is too high.
- **Model papers and cards** mention the optimizer, often "AdamW", a variant of Adam.
- **Hyperparameters:** settings you choose before training, like the learning rate, as opposed to parameters the
  model learns.

## Common confusions

- **Gradient descent doesn't find the perfect answer.** It finds a good enough low point. For neural networks, that
  is usually plenty.
- **Parameters vs hyperparameters:** the model learns its parameters. You pick the hyperparameters.
- **Gradient descent vs backpropagation:** backpropagation computes the gradients; gradient descent uses them to
  update the parameters.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Gradient | The direction and size of change that increases the loss; you step the opposite way |
| Gradient descent | Repeatedly stepping parameters in the direction that lowers the loss |
| Learning rate | How big each step is |
| Batch | A small group of examples used for one step |
| Epoch | One full pass through the training data |
| Optimizer | The exact update method, such as SGD or Adam |
