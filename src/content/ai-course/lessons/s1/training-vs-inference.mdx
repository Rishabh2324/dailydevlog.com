## Why this matters

"Why doesn't ChatGPT remember what I told it yesterday?" "Why does training cost millions but an API call costs a
fraction of a cent?" Both questions have the same answer: training and inference are completely different phases.

By the end of this lesson, you'll know the difference and why it matters for cost, speed and behavior.

## The idea

A model has two phases in its life:

- **Training** is where the model learns. It runs gradient descent over huge amounts of data and adjusts its
  parameters. It happens once (or occasionally), takes a long time and costs a lot.
- **Inference** is where the model is used. You send an input, it runs a forward pass, and it returns an answer. The
  parameters stay fixed. It happens millions of times, each call is fast and cheap.

```text
TRAINING (once, slow, costly)      INFERENCE (every request)
data → adjust parameters     →     frozen model: input → answer
```

## A real-life example

Think of a **doctor**. Medical school is training: years of study, very expensive, done before seeing patients.
Seeing a patient is inference: the doctor uses what they learned to give a diagnosis in minutes. Seeing one patient
doesn't retrain the doctor's whole education.

A real case: Meta reported that pretraining its Llama 2 family took about **3.3 million GPU hours** in total
(Touvron et al., 2023). Using one of those models to answer a question takes a few seconds on one machine.

## How it works

1. **Training** uses the forward pass, the loss, the backward pass and gradient descent, over and over (lessons
   1.1.6 to 1.1.8).
2. **When training ends**, the parameters are saved. This saved file is "the model", sometimes called a checkpoint.
3. **Inference** loads those parameters and runs only the forward pass. No loss, no backward pass, no updates.
4. **Because weights are frozen**, the model doesn't learn from your conversations. A chatbot seems to "remember"
   earlier messages only because the app sends the whole conversation again each time (lesson 2.4.2).
5. **To change the model's behavior** you either train again, fine-tune it on new examples (lesson 8.2.1), or give
   it better instructions and information in the input.

## Where you'll see it

- **API pricing** is inference pricing: you pay per request, measured in tokens.
- **"Knowledge cutoff"** is the date the training data ends. The model knows nothing after it (lesson 1.4.4).
- **Inference speed** (tokens per second, time to first token) matters for user experience.
- **Hardware:** training needs big GPU clusters; inference can run on a single GPU, and small models even on a
  laptop or phone.

## Common confusions

- **Chatting with a model doesn't train it.** Providers may use conversations to train future versions, but your
  current model doesn't change during your chat.
- **Inference isn't free just because training is over.** For a popular product, total inference cost can grow
  larger than the training cost.
- **Fine-tuning is training.** It's a smaller, cheaper round of training on top of an existing model.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Training | The phase where a model learns its parameters from data |
| Inference | Using a trained model to produce answers |
| Frozen weights | Parameters that don't change during inference |
| Checkpoint | A saved copy of a model's parameters |
| Fine-tuning | Extra training on top of an existing model |
